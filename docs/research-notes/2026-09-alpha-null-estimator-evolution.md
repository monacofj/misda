# Evolution of the `alpha_null` estimator

- Period: August–September 2026
- Status: **Adopted later; previous rule superseded**
- Normative outcome: ADR 0015 supersedes ADR 0010
- Tracking: issue #44

## Question

MISDA needs a data-driven null reference for deciding when observed positive correlation is stronger than accidental correlation expected under independence. An early design summarized null permutations by the **mean of their maximum positive pairwise correlations** and used a sequential `mean ± MC-SE` convergence rule with a `10N` safety cap.

That design was statistically plausible but had to be tested against the estimator's actual role in graph construction.

## Failure mode discovered

Repeated clean runs of the fully independent controlled case exposed a structural problem. The observed dataset is itself one realization of the same independence regime represented by the permutations. Its largest accidental correlation can therefore exceed the *mean* of the permutation maxima with non-negligible frequency.

For an ordinary hypothesis summary that might be tolerable. For MISDA graph construction, however, one exceedance creates a graph edge. A single false positive can reduce the independence number and therefore alter `dl` or `ds`.

The relevant null question was thus not “is this above the typical null maximum?” but “is this above the empirical range of maxima generated by the null experiment?”

## Alternatives examined

The investigation considered the behavior of the sequential mean/MC-SE rule, the effect of its runtime cap, and larger fixed permutation budgets. It also checked whether a separate absolute-correlation null calibration would materially change dimensional conclusions.

The empirically stronger alternative was the null envelope:

```text
B = N
r_null = max(m_1, ..., m_N)
```

where each `m_b` is the maximum positive pairwise correlation from one independent-column permutation.

Increasing the envelope budget from `N` to `2N`, `5N`, or `10N` did not change dimensional conclusions in the tested battery. Shared positive-null calibration versus separate positive/absolute calibration likewise did not change those tested dimensional conclusions.

## Outcome

The sequential mean-of-maxima estimator, its MC-SE stopping rule, and the `10N` cap were superseded. The fixed `B=N` empirical envelope became normative in ADR 0015.

Validation recorded for the adopted rule included complete recovery across repeated clean independence runs and the regular controlled cases, with the known transitive-chain and regime-switching failures remaining visible rather than being hidden by the threshold change.

## Why keep this note

ADR 0015 states the current rule. This note preserves the scientific reason the previous rule was abandoned. Without that history, a future optimization could easily “improve efficiency” by reverting from an envelope to a mean or percentile and inadvertently restore the exact false-edge mechanism that motivated the change.
